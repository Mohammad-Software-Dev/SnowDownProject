# Snowdown — Environment Art Direction (LOCKED)

Status: LOCKED for prototype art direction. Revisit only after playtest evidence or explicit user instruction.

## Master direction
Snowdown takes place in a stylized prehistoric/fantastical frozen wilderness: an animated-film-quality world sculpted by glaciers, snow, ancient rock, frozen rivers and enormous ice formations. The goal is an ownable frozen-world identity, not a generic ski resort and not a military shooter covered in snow.

## Composition formula
- ~70% spectacular natural frozen wilderness
- ~20% small warm human outposts / shelters / traversal structures
- ~10% vegetation, props and environmental accents

These percentages describe visual emphasis, not literal map-area quotas.

## Core visual pillars
1. Ice-Age-scale natural formations: glacier walls, arches, overhangs, caves, frozen canyons and dramatic mountain silhouettes.
2. Stylized rather than photorealistic: simplified, readable shapes and controlled materials.
3. Cold-vs-warm language: competitive wilderness is predominantly blue/cyan/white; human/social/safe landmarks use restrained warm amber light, wood and fabric accents.
4. Gameplay-first geography: every major formation should contribute to navigation, cover, sightlines, high ground, flanking or sliding—not exist only as scenery.
5. Competitive readability: player silhouettes, incoming snowballs, routes and cover must remain readable against snow.
6. Surface language: snow, hard ice, slideable ice, rock and inaccessible terrain must be visually distinguishable.
7. Weather is atmosphere, not visual obstruction: light drifting snow and localized powder are preferred; heavy blizzards are not default competitive conditions.

## Gameplay/environment vocabulary
Preferred recurring elements:
- giant glacier formations and natural ice arches
- ice caves as flank/shortcut routes
- frozen rivers/lakes as exposed traversal spaces and possible movement modifiers
- snowbanks/ridges as ramps and line-of-sight breaks
- boulders/ice pillars as hard cover
- sparse pines to frame spaces, not dense forests that destroy sightlines
- small rugged outposts, bridges, ropes, banners, lanterns and shelters
- strong landmark silhouettes for orientation

## First-map direction
The first production-quality arena should be a Glacier Valley / Glacier Outpost concept: a contained competitive arena inside a huge frozen landscape, with one modest warm human outpost integrated into the natural ice world.

The first vertical slice should use only enough of this language to validate gameplay. Do NOT block the prototype on elaborate waterfalls, auroras, geothermal systems, gondolas, dynamic blizzards, ancient ruins, large settlements or multiple biomes.

## Reference lessons
External references are directional only. Do not copy copyrighted characters, architecture, layouts, textures or distinctive assets.

- Stylized glacier/environment work: TAKE large readable frozen shapes, scale and stylization. DO NOT copy exact formations.
- Ice-cave references / Destiny Europa: TAKE material depth, cyan cave lighting, snow accumulation and readable ice. DO NOT take sci-fi architecture, realism or serious tone.
- Knockout City / Overwatch competitive maps: TAKE visual hierarchy, landmarking, route readability and clear combat spaces. DO NOT take their visual identity.
- Riders Republic / Steep: TAKE terrain flow, inviting slopes and movement readability. DO NOT take realistic rendering/open-world scale.
- Fortnite snow/ice examples: TAKE player-to-cover scale and immediately distinguishable frozen surfaces. DO NOT use Fortnite as Snowdown's art-style target.
- Warm winter settlement references: TAKE cold exterior vs warm shelter contrast. Avoid Christmas-specific or overly cute theming.
- Splitgate 2 Glacier production reference: TAKE the concept-to-competitive-level workflow. DO NOT take sci-fi architecture.

## Explicit exclusions
Snowdown environment should NOT read as:
- military warfare
- survival-horror frozen wasteland
- photorealistic ski simulator
- Christmas-themed game
- Fortnite clone
- dense forest shooter
- dark sci-fi ice planet

## Astra implementation rule
Treat this document as the authoritative environment art-direction brief. Optimize the first playable arena for movement, throwing, dodging, sightlines and orientation before visual spectacle. When visual references conflict with gameplay readability, gameplay readability wins.
