# SNOWDOWN — MAP 01: GLACIER VALLEY / GLACIER OUTPOST — LOCKED

Status: **LOCKED / AUTHORITATIVE FIRST-MAP DIRECTION**

This document supersedes the older provisional "Alpine Village" concept as the first production-quality competitive map direction.

## Map Identity

**Working name:** Glacier Valley  
**Secondary name:** Glacier Outpost

Map 1 is a contained 4v4 competitive arena inside a huge fantastical frozen wilderness.

The playable space should feel small enough for constant engagement, while the surrounding glaciers, cliffs and mountains imply a much larger world.

The map combines:
- Ice-Age-scale natural glacier formations;
- snow-covered rock;
- a small warm human outpost;
- one major ice cave flank route;
- one frozen-river / sliding route;
- layered high and low ground;
- multiple snow sources;
- natural cover built from terrain rather than military barricades.

The arena should feel like a **winter playground shaped by nature**, not a military combat arena.

---

## Core Gameplay Goal

The map must support:

**move → read → throw → dodge/catch → replenish → reposition**

Every major region should create at least one meaningful decision between:
- speed vs cover;
- high ground vs exposure;
- direct route vs flank;
- long-range throw vs close engagement;
- immediate pressure vs replenishing snowballs.

The map should make Snowdown's movement and projectile systems feel better, not merely display the environment art direction.

---

## Target Player Count

**LOCKED TARGET:** 4v4 / 8 players.

The map should first be balanced for eight players.

Do not enlarge the arena to support hypothetical 12–20 player modes before 4v4 is proven fun.

---

## Prototype Footprint

Exact dimensions remain **PROVISIONAL / DATA-DRIVEN**, but blockout should begin approximately in this range:

- playable long axis: ~100–130 m;
- playable short axis: ~70–100 m;
- meaningful vertical difference: roughly 12–22 m from lowest common route to highest normal combat perch.

These are blockout starting values only.

Desired traversal:
- team outpost to center: roughly 8–12 seconds normal traversal;
- typical flank rotation: roughly 12–18 seconds;
- most players should encounter meaningful combat quickly without spawning directly into enemy fire.

If playtests feel empty, shrink the map before increasing mobility or projectile speed.

---

## High-Level Layout

Use a **soft three-lane structure** created naturally through terrain.

### Route A — Glacier Shelf / High Route
A higher exposed route along one side of the arena.

Purpose:
- long sightlines;
- prediction-heavy throws;
- elevated observation;
- quick access to central high ground.

Tradeoff:
- exposed silhouette;
- limited cover;
- multiple counter angles;
- no position may safely dominate both team approaches.

### Route B — Frozen River / Central Route
The broad central movement lane.

Purpose:
- fastest path into action;
- sliding/momentum opportunities;
- open medium/long-range exchanges;
- high visibility;
- visually memorable centerline.

Tradeoff:
- risky due to exposure;
- terrain ridges/ice chunks provide intermittent cover;
- players crossing should be visible from more than one direction.

### Route C — Ice Cave / Low Flank
A lower enclosed route through glacier/rock.

Purpose:
- flank;
- line-of-sight break;
- close and medium-range encounters;
- strong contrast to bright exterior spaces.

Tradeoff:
- narrower;
- fewer snow-packing opportunities inside;
- audible approach should matter;
- exits must not become unavoidable ambush traps.

Routes must reconnect in multiple places. Do not build three isolated corridors.

---

## Team Outposts

Each team starts from a modest warm outpost embedded into opposite sides of the valley.

### Visual role
Outposts communicate:
- warmth;
- safety;
- orientation;
- human presence.

Use:
- wood;
- fabric;
- lanterns;
- shelters;
- equipment storage;
- warm amber light;
- team-identification accents.

Avoid:
- giant fortified bases;
- military bunkers;
- gun-emplacement shapes;
- excessive architecture that competes with the glacier world.

### Gameplay role
Each outpost should have:
- at least two exits;
- no direct enemy sightline into initial spawn positions;
- nearby packable snow;
- rapid access to at least two primary routes;
- clear visual orientation toward the central landmark.

Outposts are not intended to be defensible fortress positions.

---

## Master Landmark

Create one dominant central environmental landmark visible from much of the map:

**The Glacier Arch**

A huge natural ice formation spanning or framing the center of the valley.

Functions:
- map orientation;
- visual identity;
- route divider/connector;
- cover;
- elevated traversal opportunity where appropriate;
- instantly recognizable screenshot silhouette.

The Glacier Arch should define the map without becoming an unbeatable high-ground fortress.

If players can stand on it:
- access must be readable;
- exposure must be significant;
- there must be at least two meaningful counters/approaches.

---

## Secondary Landmarks

Use a small number of strongly differentiated landmarks rather than clutter.

Recommended:
1. **Glacier Arch** — center/master landmark.
2. **Frozen River Bend** — fast exposed central route.
3. **Blue Ice Cave** — saturated cyan low/flank region.
4. **North/South Team Outposts** — warm orientation anchors.
5. **Icefall / Broken Glacier** — one asymmetric-looking but competitively balanced side landmark.

Names are provisional; gameplay functions are the important part.

---

## Frozen River / Sliding Route

The frozen river is a signature movement element.

Goals:
- visually obvious difference from normal snow;
- slightly faster / lower-friction traversal if the movement system supports it;
- smooth curves and slopes that make sliding intuitive;
- enough cover interruptions that it is not a pure sniper lane;
- connects meaningful regions rather than existing as decoration.

The player should look at the river and immediately think:
**"I can move fast there, but I will be exposed."**

Exact ice-friction and slide behavior remains data-driven.

---

## Ice Cave

The cave is the main strong flank route.

Visual rule:
- exterior = white / pale blue / open sky;
- cave = deeper saturated cyan/blue, darker values and translucent ice.

Gameplay requirements:
- two or more meaningful entrances/exits;
- never a single dead-end tunnel;
- reduced long-range dominance;
- enough width for two-way player movement;
- corners/cover without becoming a maze;
- limited or no packable snow in the deepest interior;
- clear audio cues for nearby footsteps/packing/projectiles;
- exits designed so defenders cannot safely camp them with no counter-angle.

The cave should feel useful, not mandatory.

---

## Verticality

Use approximately three readable height bands:

### Low
Frozen river, cave floor, lower snow channels.

### Mid
Most general combat terrain, snowbanks, outpost approaches.

### High
Glacier shelves, rock ledges, arch access, limited overlook positions.

Rules:
- elevated positions must trade safety for visibility/exposure;
- no perch sees both team spawns and most snow sources;
- every important high ground has at least two counters;
- avoid head-glitch geometry where only a tiny portion of the head hitbox is exposed;
- falling from ordinary combat height should not create long recovery downtime.

---

## Cover Language

Prioritize natural frozen-world cover:

- waist-height snowbanks;
- head-height snowdrifts;
- glacier chunks;
- ice pillars;
- rock outcrops;
- frozen fallen logs where appropriate;
- outpost walls/corners;
- terrain folds/ridges.

Avoid filling the map with generic crates or shooter barricades.

### Cover rhythm
A player should rarely travel more than roughly 3–5 seconds through normal contested space without access to some line-of-sight break.

Long open spaces are allowed when:
- crossing them is an intentional risk;
- alternate routes exist;
- the reward is meaningful (speed, angle, snow source, high ground).

---

## Sightline Philosophy

Snowball travel time and arc are core skill systems, so the map needs varied throw distances.

Support:
- close: cave/outpost corners;
- medium: most standard combat;
- long: river/valley/arch lanes.

Rules:
- no single dominant position controls most of the arena;
- long-range throwers must remain flankable;
- sightlines should intersect rather than form single-direction shooting galleries;
- cover should break very long uninterrupted lanes;
- terrain should support arcing throws over snowbanks and ridges.

The map should reward leading and projectile prediction without making close play irrelevant.

---

## Snow Source Placement

Snowball replenishment is part of map strategy.

Prototype target:
- from most combat zones, valid packing snow should be reachable within approximately 3–5 seconds of normal movement;
- central open snow sources should carry positional risk;
- safer snow sources may require giving up pressure/position;
- cave interior may intentionally provide little/no valid packing snow;
- team outposts should always provide nearby snow access.

Snow sources should be visible/readable without glowing gamey effects where possible.

Do not create one "ammo station" that becomes mandatory.

---

## Team Symmetry

Gameplay balance should be **functionally symmetrical**, but visuals do not need literal mirror symmetry.

Both teams should receive comparable:
- travel time to center;
- snow access;
- high-ground access;
- cave/flank options;
- cover quality;
- river access;
- spawn safety.

Natural glacier shapes can differ visually as long as competitive metrics remain comparable.

---

## Boundaries

Prefer believable natural boundaries:
- sheer glacier walls;
- deep crevasses;
- impassable snow;
- cliffs;
- rock;
- dense ice formations.

Invisible blockers are acceptable for safety, but should align with visually obvious barriers.

Do not place arbitrary invisible walls across surfaces that look traversable.

---

## Out-of-Bounds Recovery

If a player falls into a crevasse or leaves playable bounds:
- use a quick reset/return;
- avoid death imagery;
- keep downtime minimal;
- apply short temporary protection if needed;
- prevent deliberate OOB abuse.

Exact score/streak consequence remains provisional.

---

## Environmental Motion / Weather

For Map 1:
- light drifting snow allowed;
- subtle blowing powder from ridges allowed;
- localized ice mist allowed;
- small environmental motion allowed.

Do not include during the vertical slice:
- heavy blizzard;
- avalanche simulation;
- major dynamic destruction;
- flooding;
- elaborate weather transitions;
- large animated wildlife;
- aurora systems;
- geothermal biome systems.

Competitive visibility always wins.

---

## Map Art Priority

Blockout and gameplay validation come before final glacier art.

Development order:
1. graybox terrain/route network;
2. player-scale movement validation;
3. snow source placement;
4. projectile/sightline validation;
5. 4v4 playtest;
6. telemetry review;
7. route/cover adjustments;
8. landmark art pass;
9. material/lighting pass;
10. polish/VFX.

Astra must not spend major time sculpting beautiful glaciers before the blockout plays well.

## Metrics to Collect

During multiplayer testing collect, where practical:

- throws heatmap;
- successful hits heatmap;
- times-hit heatmap;
- head-hit lanes;
- average throw distance;
- snow-source usage;
- average distance/time to snow source;
- cave usage;
- river usage;
- high-ground occupancy;
- catch frequency by region;
- player congestion;
- time to first meaningful engagement;
- traversal time between landmarks;
- out-of-bounds attempts;
- team-side win rate.

If one region dominates these metrics, investigate geometry before changing core combat balance.

---

## Acceptance Criteria — Blockout

Map 1 blockout is ready for multiplayer testing when:

1. Both teams can reach meaningful central combat in roughly comparable time.
2. Each team has at least two viable exits/approaches.
3. Glacier Arch is a clear orientation landmark.
4. Frozen River provides a useful fast/exposed route.
5. Ice Cave provides a viable flank route without becoming mandatory.
6. High ground has multiple counters.
7. Most combat spaces have nearby line-of-sight breaks.
8. Snow sources are available without eliminating resource decisions.
9. No team can safely attack directly into the opposing spawn.
10. Long, medium and close projectile engagements all occur naturally.
11. Eight players can occupy the arena without constant congestion or long periods without contact.
12. No single location obviously controls the majority of the map.

---

## Explicit Scope Guard

Map 1 does **not** need:
- multiple biomes;
- a full resort/village;
- moving gondolas;
- cinematic waterfalls;
- avalanche simulation;
- extensive interiors;
- advanced destructibility;
- wildlife;
- dynamic weather systems;
- dozens of buildings;
- production-quality art before playtest.

The first goal is a great 4v4 snowball arena inside a memorable glacier valley.

---

## Final Map Fantasy

**Eight realistic winter-clad players having an intense, playful snowball fight inside an enormous frozen valley — racing across ice, diving behind snowbanks, flanking through glowing blue ice caves, arcing throws beneath a colossal glacier arch, and regrouping near small warm outposts.**

The world should feel spectacular.

The playable geometry should feel immediately understandable.

The combat should feel like Snowdown.
